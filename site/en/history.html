About us · 1970 — 2026

History

From the Leningrad school of acoustics and sound systems for aviation and space to our own concert loudspeaker brand, with fully localized manufacturing in St. Petersburg and the Leningrad Region.

Scroll down
CHAPTER 01
1970s–1980s

How it all began

In the 1970s and 80s, Leningrad was one of the leading centers of acoustics research in the USSR. Industrial enterprises were at work (the Krasnaya Zarya research and production association, Radiopribor, the Vibrator and Radiotekhnika plants, the Akustika design bureau), and leading universities ran research programs: Leningrad State University, the Polytechnic Institute, LETI (the Electrotechnical Institute) and the Institute of Cinema Engineers. It was at LETI, under the supervision of Professor Irina Aldoshina, that the company’s future founder Yuri Starchenko studied. Alongside his studies he repaired loudspeakers and sound systems, building up hands-on experience.

CHAPTER 02
1990s

EVM: Russian loudspeakers on the world market

In the late 1980s and early 1990s, a small group of like-minded people, among them Starchenko, grew into a large-scale manufacturer of professional loudspeakers and sound systems. Its products were exported to Europe and the USA, with customers including Genelec, McCauley and BMS.

That company was EVM, a brand known in the 90s to practically everyone in the pro audio business. EVM was replacing imports with domestic products long before anyone talked about sanctions; it exported actively and had offices in Los Angeles (EVM-USA) and Nice (EVM-France). Back then, “made in Russia” really meant made, not just assembled. Production of the first branded drivers was set up as a joint venture with specialists from the American company McCauley, which went on to become a regular EVM customer. The legendary Genelec 1035A studio monitor was built with Russian drivers, and a collaboration with the German HF driver maker BMS added LF and MF drivers to the BMS range, drivers that world-famous companies such as L-Acoustics use to this day.

But in the late 1990s the company, which by then held substantial assets, fell victim to a hostile corporate raid and effectively ceased to exist as Russia’s largest loudspeaker manufacturer. Starchenko started over with a new company of his own.

1990s — 2000

On the same stage as the global brands

Trade shows in Frankfurt and at NAMM in Los Angeles, meetings with the owners of BMS and Fender, a visit to Cerwin-Vega’s closed lab. For many years, Yuri’s company was the partner and exclusive distributor of BMS in Russia.

CHAPTER 03
2000s

Special technologies

In the early 2000s, major European and American manufacturers moved aggressively into the Russian concert equipment market, and by the end of the decade small Russian companies, including the new company founded by Starchenko, had largely lost the battle for market leadership. The response was a change of strategy: under the leadership of a young engineer, Nikolay Milkov , an engineering department was set up to focus on niche specialized sound systems: from sound simulation for aviation and space flight simulators (Mi-17, Mi-26 and Ka-52 helicopters, the Sukhoi Superjet 100 aircraft, and the training complex at the Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Center in Star City) to a public address system for the Mi-8, one of the noisiest helicopters, and equipment for the Emergencies Ministry, the Ministry of Defense, the Interior Ministry and the National Guard.

CHAPTER 04
2013

The birth of SST

By 2013, building on their work in specialized equipment, Starchenko, Milkov and Baranov founded Special Sound Technologies (SST). The engineering department concentrated on special projects, and concert loudspeaker output shrank to one-off orders. Built on technology developed by Milkov are mass notification systems that cover areas of over 10 km² from a single point, long-range public address stations, bird deterrent systems and ship horns. From the mid-2010s, the company began designing acoustic test chambers for strength testing of spacecraft components, a narrow niche where it managed to edge out European and American manufacturers. Today this expertise is backed by several patents and by projects accepted to military acceptance standards. Since 2022, SST has fully localized production of every driver it uses, including HF compression drivers and 4″ to 18″ cone drivers.

More than a decade of work for the Ministry of Defense, Roscosmos and other government clients, where there is no room for error, shaped the standards of discipline and quality control that later became the foundation of DS PROAUDIO.

CHAPTER 05
2019

The birth of DS PROAUDIO

Accumulated experience, a production base and a close-knit team led Milkov, Starchenko and Baranov to the idea of reviving concert equipment production at a new level. That called for a partner with a strong position in the pro audio market, not tied to any existing brand, who shared their confidence that Russian solutions could compete and was ready to bring new products to market from scratch together.

“In the summer of 2019, Special Sound Technologies set itself a goal: to restore series production of professional loudspeaker systems. … Almost immediately our attention was drawn to Deep Sound, a fast-growing company led by a young and passionate manager.”Nikolay Milkov, Technical Director of SST

In the spring and summer of 2019, SST approached Deep Sound with this proposal. Deep Sound had been founded in 2009 by Vyacheslav Motsar and had already earned a reputation as a reliable partner for equipping cultural, sports and educational venues with sound systems. At that moment Deep Sound was itself looking to move from installation work to its own loudspeaker brand, and after brief negotiations the two sides agreed: SST and Deep Sound, while remaining two independent companies, joined forces in a joint venture, DS PROAUDIO. Motsar defined the brand’s name and direction; Milkov provided product development and the production base.

By then, Deep Sound’s specialists had helped prepare Luzhniki Stadium for the FIFA World Cup, completed a 1,000-seat concert hall at the Mytishchi branch of Bauman Moscow State Technical University, and delivered sports venues for the Moscow Sports Department, including the training rink of figure skating champions.

“We considered foreign partners, and we also had offers from Russian manufacturers. But in SST we saw, for the first time, a real willingness to work together: they listened (and actually heard us!) about what the product should be, what specifications were needed, and which price segment to target.”Vyacheslav Motsar, head of Deep Sound

2019

Gazprom Arena

In 2019, the same year the DS PROAUDIO brand was taking shape, SST equipped Gazprom Arena in St. Petersburg with a public address system built on AI300 horn loudspeakers. You can still see it today when driving past on the Western High-Speed Diameter: on one of the pylons, right where the cables that support the stadium roof fan out.

CHAPTER 06
2020–2021

From compact systems to the first patent

The target was the project (installed sound) segment of the pro audio market, with a clear understanding that competing with Chinese manufacturers on price was not an option. Development went from the bottom up: it started with the compact ANT and CXseries, to win the trust of the first users in small formats, from premium home karaoke to school assembly halls. These series already featured the brand’s signature coaxial design for full-range loudspeakers, which delivers the most consistent directivity across the HF/LF crossover region and has been used in every more complex design since.

The first big milestone was the ERA series, a scalable constant-curvature array built from point-source elements. The final coaxial solution proved original enough to be patented, and after its showing at the Music Moscow trade show in 2021 it won praise from the most demanding part of the concert industry. It was while working on ERA that the first principles of the DS PROAUDIO philosophy took shape: deliver undistorted music to as many listeners in the room as possible, put consistent tonal balance ahead of equal loudness, and reproduce sound the way the musicians and engineers intended it rather than “improving” it with a sound of our own.

CHAPTER 07
2022

The test of 2022 and full localization

2022 was a turning point for the industry. The departure of Western brands sharply raised interest in Russian manufacturers, but it also confronted DS PROAUDIO with a serious challenge: the company lost its supply of German HF drivers. The answer was to design its own HF compression driver and put it into series production within a single year. Since then, every driver in DS PROAUDIO products, from 4″ to 18″, has been made in-house in St. Petersburg. The same year, the company signed a distribution agreement with AUVIX, one of the largest players on the market, and its partner network grew several times over.

CHAPTER 08
2023

The year of scaling up

2023 was the year of scaling up: new workshops opened, equipment was purchased, and the production team and output grew roughly 2.5 times. At the ProLight & Sound show, the ERA series headlined the range for the third time, and it became clear that the market needed a fundamentally new, larger system.

CHAPTER 09
2024

A new flagship

In 2024, at an industry trade show, DS PROAUDIO unveiled the TAURA series: a line array with coaxially arranged transducers, whose design is also protected by a patent.

CHAPTER 10
2025

One site and major festivals

In 2025, the component workshops were brought together on a single site in St. Petersburg (the series enclosure shop operates in the Leningrad Region), factory tours for partners were launched, and the TAURA line array was heard for the first time at major summer music festivals, including Dikaya Myata and Bessonnitsa (see feedback in Testimonials). The company continues to take part in industry trade shows and conferences.

CHAPTER 11
2026

Today

In 2026, DS PROAUDIO once again presented its systems at Light + Audio Tec in Moscow under the motto “Live the music! Where engineering meets art.” Everything on the stand was designed and built in St. Petersburg and the Leningrad Region.
