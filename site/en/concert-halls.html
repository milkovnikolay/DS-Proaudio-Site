Challenge and solution

Natural sound for orchestra and voice

Audiences come to a concert hall for live sound. Poor reinforcement colors the voice, fights the room’s acoustics and is immediately audible, especially in chamber programs.

We model the hall before delivery, choose a system that suits its acoustics and tune it on site together with the technical staff.

  • See the result before you buy: we model the room in EASE and show you the SPL map before delivery.
  • Reinforcement supports the hall’s acoustics rather than replacing them.
  • Venue staff can run the system without a hired sound engineer: we tune it on site together with your technical team.
About the solution

Equipment that stays true to the composer’s intent. For classical, jazz and chamber music, with maximum intelligibility and natural timbre.

What makes the venue different

Why does a concert hall need a special approach?

Unlike a club or a stadium, people come to a philharmonic hall for the art of performance. Overwhelming loudness isn’t the goal; what you need is:

pp ff
Micro-dynamics
The system conveys the finest nuances of a pianissimo: quiet phrases don’t get lost or squashed.
L R stage depth
Stage and depth
Accurate placement of the orchestra’s instruments: listeners hear where each section sits.
No coloration
The system sounds neutral and adds nothing of its own: piano, voice and strings keep their natural timbre.
System architecture

What we recommend for the hall

The choice depends on how well the hall is acoustically treated.

Low- or mid-power line arrays
Even coverage of the stalls and balconies without level jumps.
Line arrays →
Low-profile stage monitors
Front fills for soloists: performers hear themselves naturally, without straining.
Stage monitors →
Fast-response subwoofers
Double bass and the piano’s low register without boom.
Powerful column loudspeakers as the main system
Narrow vertical directivity with even coverage of the stalls: the sound goes to the seats, not the ceiling and walls.
